# E1 — Where does decode time go? (2026-10-02)

**Setup:** `experiments/e1_decode.py`. Random dataset, 64-token prompts, 256 output tokens with
`--ignore-eos`, 4 warmups, `max(16, 4×c)` requests per point. Configs: `default`,
`no_async` (`--no-async-scheduling`), `eager` (`--enforce-eager`). Async scheduling is on by
default in this version (`vllm/config/vllm.py`). Zero preemptions in all runs.

## Findings
1. **Bandwidth-bound to c≈16.** TPOT +4% from c=1→16 while throughput ×15. Weights are read
   once per step regardless of batch size.
2. **TPOT growth at c=32–64 is mostly KV-read traffic, not compute.** At c=64 each step also
   reads ~64 × ~190 tokens × 144 KiB ≈ 1.8 GB of KV ≈ 2 ms of bandwidth — about half of the
   +3.7 ms. Throughput is still near-linear (46× at c=64), so compute is not saturated.
   ⇒ Longer contexts should push TPOT up quickly (test in E1b; motivates E5 FP8 KV).
   *Caveats (review):* the 46× is just c × TPOT ratio, not independent evidence about compute.
   And compute isn't far off: c=64 is ~1 TFLOP/step, and if GeForce FP8 with FP32 accumulate is
   half rate (to confirm) that is ~3–6 ms of GEMM vs the ~9.7 ms memory floor. The other half of
   +3.7 ms (GEMM efficiency at larger M, per-request sampling over 152k vocab, scheduler) is unattributed.
3. **CUDA graphs + torch.compile ≈ 8 ms/token, flat across batch size** → CPU/kernel-launch
   overhead, not GPU work. Eager = 61% of default throughput at c=1, 73% at c=64.
   *Note:* `--enforce-eager` disables **both** torch.compile and CUDA graphs
   (`vllm/config/vllm.py:1689`), so ~8 ms is their combined effect. To split them, add a config
   with `-cc.cudagraph_mode=none` only. Gap shrinks to 6.3 ms at c=64 (launches hide behind
   larger GPU work). WSL2 GPU paravirtualization likely inflates launch cost.
4. **Async scheduling: 0.3–0.9 ms/token (2.5–5%)**, growing with batch size as per-step
   scheduler work grows.
5. **Unexplained overhead at c=1 ≈ 2 ms (~16%).** Floor: ~8.2 GB read per step (8.8 GiB weights
   minus the 1.24 GB embedding table, which is only gathered) ÷ 896 GB/s ≈ 9.2 ms at peak;
   ~10.3 ms at a realistic 85–90% of peak. Measured 12.3 ms.
   **Update:** measured read bandwidth is 849 GB/s (`bwtest.py`) ⇒ floor **9.7 ms**, gap **~2.6 ms (21%)**.
   *(Correction: an earlier quick estimate said 9.8 ms floor from 8.8 GB; 9.2 ms is the better number.)*

**Caveat:** TTFT in E1 is mostly queueing behind decode steps with small sample sizes (16–256
requests/point) — don't read into small TTFT differences (e.g. no_async looking better at c=32).
E2 is the TTFT experiment. Prompts also got unintended prefix-cache hits across sweep points
(see [E1b caveat](E1b.md#caveat--prefix-cache-hits-in-random-workloads-1173)).
